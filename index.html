Who we are

An adventure club with a job to do.

Dravaka started with a handful of divers who were tired of surfacing with a mesh bag full of somebody else’s rubbish. We decided the trip and the clean-up should be the same outing.

So that is what we run. We teach people to dive and snorkel, we take them up hills and out onto rivers, and every programme carries a share of the work that keeps those places alive: waste management, pollution control, coral regeneration, beach clean-ups and education for the towns we operate from. Reduce, reuse and recycle is not a poster in our office — it is the reason the club exists.

You do not need experience, gear or a cause already picked out. You need a free weekend and a willingness to carry something back up the beach.

Join & membership programmes

What a Dravaka membership actually means

Four questions everyone asks before they sign up. Here are the honest answers.

What’s our role?

We run the trips, hold the safety standards, and organise the conservation work that follows every season. Members get first call on both.

Our impact

18.4 tonnes of waste off beaches and seabeds, 2,600 mangrove saplings in the ground, 340 coral fragments nursed and out-planted.

How it helps the environment

Clean water column, replanted mangrove belt, recovering reef, and a coastal town that earns from keeping it that way rather than stripping it.

What we’re doing

Monthly beach clean-ups, mangrove cultivation, coral nurseries, plastic-waste segregation and school sessions on all of it.

What we are doing

Four things we work on, all year

Small, repeatable work that adds up. Each one changes something specific about the water you swim in.

Ocean ecosystem

Plastic-free ocean

Floating plastic breaks into pieces small enough for fish to eat. We intercept it at the shoreline, before the tide takes it out.

Read more
Beach clean-up

Beach clean-ups

First Saturday of every month, six bases, sorted and weighed on the sand so we know what is actually washing up.

Read more
Waste management

Segregate, reuse, recycle

Everything we collect gets sorted. Glass and metal go to local recyclers; nets and rope come back as gear ties and mats.

Read more
Education

Awareness sessions

Free sessions for schools and fishing cooperatives on tides, waste and what a healthy reef pays back to a coastal town.

Our missions

A plastic-free coast, one Saturday at a time.

Six programmes, six bases, run on a schedule rather than a mood. Here is what each one is, why we do it, and how you get on the boat.

Mission statement

What we are, plainly

Dravaka is an adventure club that funds and staffs conservation work on the Indian coast. We teach people to dive, snorkel, trek and camp — and we use the money and the people that generates to clear waste from the water, replant mangrove, rebuild reef and teach coastal communities why it pays to keep it that way.

The environment is not a cause we adopted for marketing. It is the thing our entire business stands on. A reef with no fish is a dive site nobody books. A beach under plastic is a camp nobody wants. Protecting it is not charity for us — it is maintenance, and we would rather do it in public with anyone who wants to help.

The goal: a plastic-free stretch of coast at every base we operate, with a mangrove belt behind it and a reef in front of it that is measurably better each year than the one before.

Why Dravaka does this

Because we are already there. We have boats, compressors, trained divers and a permit to be in the water at six locations, every week, for eight months of the year. Nobody else on that coast has that combination sitting idle between trips. Not using it would be a waste.

And because the work is genuinely small enough to do. Nobody is going to fix the ocean. But 40 people can strip a kilometre of beach in three hours, and a rope nursery costs less than a set of regulators. Those things are within reach, so we do them.

The programmes

Six missions

Mission 01

Plastic-free ocean

Most of the plastic in the sea did not arrive by boat. It came down a river or blew off a beach, and it is recoverable for a short window before the tide takes it out and the sun breaks it into pieces too small to catch.

We work that window. Shoreline interception at all six bases, underwater collection dives on the reef sites, and a standing arrangement with two river panchayats to clear the estuary mouths before monsoon.

Effect: less fragmentation into microplastic, fewer ingestion deaths in reef fish and turtles, and a water column clear enough for coral to get light.

Mission 02

Beach clean-ups

First Saturday of every month, 6:30 am, all six bases. Open to anyone — members, guests, schools, people who saw the sign. We supply gloves, bags, a scale and breakfast.

Everything gets sorted and weighed on the sand before it leaves, so we know what is actually washing up rather than guessing. That record is what lets us argue with the people upstream.

Effect: 18.4 tonnes recovered to date; dune grasses re-rooting at Kaup and Kovalam where the debris line used to sit.

Mission 03

Waste management — reduce, reuse, recycle

Collecting waste is the easy half. The hard half is making sure it does not go straight into a pit or a fire behind the village.

Glass, metal and PET go to two local recyclers we pay directly. Ghost net and rope comes back to us and gets remade into gear ties, boat mats and the bags we hand out at the next clean-up. What genuinely cannot be recycled is logged, so the number is visible rather than quietly buried.

Effect: no open burning at our bases, and a reuse rate we publish every season instead of a claim we make.

Mission 04

Mangrove cultivation

Mangrove is the cheapest coastal defence there is and the fastest carbon sink per hectare in the country. It also happens to be the nursery that most of the reef fish we dive with grow up in.

We raise saplings in two nurseries, plant with the local panchayats along estuary banks, and come back for three seasons to check survival. Planting without the follow-up is theatre; we count what is still standing at year three.

Effect: 2,600 saplings planted, 71% surviving at three years, and a creek bank at Aghanashini that held through last monsoon.

Mission 05

Coral regeneration

Storms and anchors break coral off the reef. Loose fragments usually die on the sand. Fixed to a rope or frame nursery, a good proportion of them live and grow fast enough to be planted back within a year or two.

We run nurseries off Netrani and Kaup, maintained on scheduled dives by divemasters and members who have done our reef-survey speciality. It is slow, repetitive, and one of the few conservation activities where you can point at the result.

Effect: 340 fragments nursed, 210 out-planted, with branching Acropora taking best on the shallower frames.

Mission 06

Educational awareness

Every clean-up we run is a temporary fix unless the town behind the beach has a reason to care about it. So we teach.

Free sessions for schools, fishing cooperatives and resort staff covering waste segregation, tide and current safety, reef health, and the straightforward economics of a coastline that people still want to visit. Around 4,100 students so far.

Effect: three schools running their own monthly clean-ups without us, which is the actual goal.

Best time to dive, coast by coast

Updated for the current season·6 min read

India has two diving seasons running on opposite calendars. Book the wrong coast for the month and you will spend the trip watching the sea from a window.

West coast — Karnataka and Kerala

Open roughly October to May, best between December and March. Visibility at Netrani runs 12–25 m in that window and can drop under 5 m either side of it. June to September is monsoon: the boats do not go out, and anyone telling you otherwise is taking a risk with your money and your safety.

East coast — Pondicherry and Tamil Nadu

The mirror image. Open February to September, best from May to August when the west is shut. The north-east monsoon closes it from October to January.

Lakshadweep

October to May, with the flattest water from January to April. Permits take about two weeks to arrange and we do that with your booking — do not plan a trip inside a fortnight.

Andaman

November to April. December to February is peak for a reason: 30 m visibility is common at Dixon’s and Johnny’s Gorge. It is also when the flights and the dive slots sell out, so book two to three months ahead.

Months to avoid entirely

  • June–August, west coast. Closed. No exceptions.
  • Late October, east coast. Cyclone window; trips get cancelled at short notice.
  • Mid-May anywhere. Water is warm enough to raise bleaching risk, and we voluntarily cut nursery dives.

One rule that beats all of the above: book two days at a site, not one. Conditions turn. A single-day trip that gets blown out is a wasted journey; a two-day trip almost always gets you in the water.

Camping: what our camps are actually like

Beach and forest camps·4 min read

Two-person tents, a shared kitchen, a composting toilet and no generator. If you were hoping for glamping, this is not that.

What we provide

  • Tent, sleeping mat and a liner (bring your own bag in winter)
  • All meals, cooked on site — vegetarian by default, fish on the coast if you want it
  • Drinking water from a filter unit, not bottles
  • First aid kit, satellite messenger on forest camps, and a guide who knows the route

What you bring

  • A refillable bottle. We do not sell or supply single-use plastic on any trip.
  • Closed shoes, a head torch, and warm layers — the Ghats get cold after dark
  • Any personal medication, declared on the form

The waste rule

Everything that comes in goes out, including organic waste on dune and beach sites where burying it just feeds crows. We weigh the pack-out at the end of every camp and it goes into the same log as the clean-ups. Nobody enjoys this part; everybody does it.

Booking

Camps run ₹2,200 per person per night, minimum two nights, groups of 6–14. Monsoon camps in the Ghats are the ones worth doing — wet, green, and almost empty.

Trekking update: route changes this season

Western Ghats·3 min read

Three changes worth knowing before you book a Ghats trek with us this season.

Kudremukh — permits now capped daily

The forest department has capped daily entries. We hold a block of permits per weekend, but they go early. Book at least three weeks out, and give us your ID details at booking rather than on the morning.

Kumara Parvatha — second-day start moved earlier

We now leave the Bhattaru Mane camp at 5:00 am rather than 6:30. The last two seasons the afternoon heat on the open ridge caused more trouble than the climb did. It makes for a hard wake-up and a much better day.

Netravali — open again

Closed last season after landslides on the approach. The route is repaired and we are running it from November. It stays our best beginner overnight: 9 km, gentle gradient, water at the halfway point.

Leeches. June through September, on every Ghats route, without exception. Salt, tobacco or a leech sock — pick one and stop worrying about it. They are harmless and they are not going anywhere.

Equipment: what to buy, what to hire, what to skip

Dive kit·5 min read

New divers are usually sold the expensive things first. Here is the order we would actually buy in.

Buy early

  • Mask. The single piece of kit where fit matters more than price. A leaking rental mask ruins more dives than anything else. ₹2,500–5,000.
  • Snorkel and boots. Cheap, personal, and unpleasant to share.
  • Logbook. Certification bodies want logged dives before your next course.

Hire for now

  • BCD and regulator. Expensive, need servicing, and the rental sets at every base are maintained and pressure-tested each season.
  • Wetsuit. Thickness depends where you dive. Try a few before committing.
  • Tanks and weights. Always included; never worth owning as a recreational diver.

Buy when you are diving 20+ times a year

  • Dive computer. The first serious purchase worth making. ₹18,000 upwards.
  • Fins. Once you know whether you prefer open heel or full foot.
  • SMB and reel. Required on any drift or boat dive; learn to deploy it before you need it.

Skip

Dive knives you will never use, camera housings before you have 30 dives of decent buoyancy, and anything sold as “reef-safe” without an ingredients list. On sunscreen specifically: if it contains oxybenzone or octinoxate, leave it at home. A rash guard does the same job and does not end up on the coral.

Servicing

Regulators annually or every 100 dives. BCD inflators yearly. Hire kit at our bases is serviced at the start of every season and pressure-tested; the log is on the wall at each dive centre if you want to look.
